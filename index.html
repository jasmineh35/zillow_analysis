A Zillow Housing Data Analysis

Location and Affordability in U.S. Housing Markets by Jasmine Hardy

  • Next

Project Background

Housing affordability has become an increasing concern, especially for first-time buyers trying to enter the market. This project looks at how accessible homes in a typical starter price range really are, and whether listing prices align with Zillow’s Zestimates.

Using data from Zillow Real Estate Listings (Top 20 U.S. States) , the analysis explores pricing patterns, the availability of lower-priced homes, and how affordability varies by location. The goal is to better understand how pricing trends and geography affect access to housing.

Research Questions

  • Do Zestimates tend to overestimate or underestimate listing prices?
  • What percentage of listings fall within a typical starter home price range?
  • Are there geographic patterns in the distribution of such homes?
  • How do average home prices vary by state?

Findings

Do Zestimates tend to overestimate or underestimate listing prices?

Zestimates show a slight tendency to underestimate listing prices, with a mean error of 0.67%. This difference is small and not statistically significant (p = 0.219), suggesting that Zestimates are generally accurate on average. However, the relatively large standard deviation (17.9%) indicates that accuracy varies across individual properties.

What percentage of listings fall within a typical starter home price range?

Up to $350,000 is often considered a typical starter home price range. In this dataset, about 26.4% of homes fall within this range, while 73.6% are priced above it. This suggests that most listings in these states may be out of reach for buyers seeking entry-level homes, highlighting limited affordability.

Are there geographic patterns in the distribution of such homes?

Starter homes do not form tight geographic clusters, but clear regional patterns exist. They are more common in parts of the Midwest and Northeast, with some presence in the South and central states, while being less common in the West. This suggests that affordability is driven more by region than by specific locations.

How do average home prices vary by state?

Average home prices vary significantly by state, even after removing the top 5% of high-end listings. States such as California and Washington remain the most expensive, with averages above $700,000, while states like Pennsylvania and Ohio are much lower, around $370,000–$400,000. This gap highlights strong regional differences and shows that location plays a major role in affordability.

Conclusion

Overall, the results show that while Zestimates are generally accurate on average, housing affordability remains a significant challenge. Most listings fall above a typical starter home price range, limiting options for buyers seeking entry-level homes. Pricing and availability also vary widely by region, with more affordable homes concentrated in specific parts of the country.

These patterns suggest that access to homeownership is shaped not only by market prices, but also by where buyers search. As a result, location plays a critical role in determining affordability, highlighting the broader challenge of uneven access to housing across the United States.
